## **Ciaran Daly @ Twisted Works**

#### **Business, Strategy & Finance**

- **General Advice:**
    - Keep the company 'lean and mean' with low overheads.
    - Cold outreach to publishers and partners can be surprisingly effective.
    - Clarity in your overall company and game strategy is essential.
    - Understand and define the company's Unique Selling Proposition (USP).
    - Market data from `SteamDB`/`SteamSpy` is standard, but often based on estimations and wishlists.
    - Understand that publisher deals may hinge on the IP rights.
    - Be prepared for soft skills to be as crucial as hard skills.
- **Actionables:**
    - Bring in external support for Legal (suggested: Sheridans, Lewis Silkens) and Accounting.
    - Investigate and apply for the **UK Games Fund**.
    - Create a comprehensive "Go to Market Plan," which is necessary even for publishers who have their own marketing teams.
    - Develop a rock-solid budget forecast that accounts for potential changes and contingencies.
    - Use `GameDiscover.co` and `Steampeek` for market/competitor research.
    - Research "Debut Title Developers" in the genre, not just direct competitors, to find comparable data.
    - Base expected returns on the average revenue of games within your genre.
    - Figure out the company's "burn rate" (monthly expenses).
    - Form an internal advisory board.
    - Find and enroll in managerial workshops or certificate programs.
    - Include a budget line item for "Soft Skills Development."

#### **Pitching & Decks**

- **General Advice:**
    - The pitch deck's primary job is to get them interested enough to play the demo; everything rides on the demo itself.
    - A pitch should be a hybrid of information about the game and the team.
    - Don't over-bloat the pitch with information.
    - Highlight what your team is good at, and be transparent about gaps and how you plan to fill them (shows credibility).
- **Actionables:**
    - Mention a contingency fund in your budget/pitch.
    - Fill in gaps in team experience with hires or advisors and show this in the deck.
    - Define and present the market opportunity clearly.
    - Create a super media-rich "10-pager" deck specifically for the Develop conference.
    - Create and maintain separate, tailored decks for both investors and publishers.
    - Send Steam codes for the demo build.

#### **Game Design & Development**

- **General Advice:**
    - Decide definitively if the game is a sandbox or mission-based experience.
    - Consider if the core experience is exclusively about dogfighting or if there are other pillars.
- **Actionables:**
    - Create more enemy variations.
    - Implement micro-objectives within playspaces.
    - Consider a timer system for playspaces to add pressure or structure.
    - Define if each level will be a "handcrafted gimmick" or follow a more systemic design.
    - Set up a Digital Ocean Virtual Machine to automatically build the game and upload new builds to a private Steamworks page.

#### **Community & Marketing**

- **General Advice:**
    - Creativity needs to be backed by credibility (show, don't just tell).
- **Actionables:**
    - Utilize leaderboards and other incentives (e.g., Twitch drops from dev streams) to draw players in.
    - Set up a game Discord server if one doesn't exist.
    - Build a social media following.
    - Capture gameplay footage at events like Q-Con.
    - Conduct playtests with your Discord community.
    - Show updates and progress regularly as part of your standard workflow.
    - Consider creating a separate "Demo" or "Prologue" page on Steam to route players to the main page.

### **Gina Jackson @ Pitchify**

#### **Business, Strategy & Finance**

- **General Advice:**
    - A £600k budget is likely too high for a debut game. Keep it low-risk.
    - Publishers typically expect a 3-4x return on their investment.
    - Understand the market thoroughly. Why is there a gap? Are similar games hard to make/market, or do they not sell?
    - Government funding bodies often look for the employment potential of a project.
    - Aim for a low-risk project that has the potential for high sales volume.
    - The "sweet spot" is being perceived as a super low-risk investment, which can give you leverage for a better royalty rate and advance.
    - Your finished game date is not your release date.
- **Actionables:**
    - Apply for the **UK Games Fund** prototype fund (£30k) or content fund (£150k, requires match funding).
    - Find trends your game can exploit.
    - Analyze genre trends where games might have mediocre Metacritic scores but high sales and player counts.
    - Prepare low, mid, and high sales predictions.
    - When asked what you need, have a clear answer for the financial advance and the royalty rate.

#### **Pitching & Decks**

- **General Advice:**
    - Be honest about the game's current state. Don't "overegg" what's there.
    - The discovery phase is valuable to publishers ("We tried X, it didn't work, and here's what we learned").
- **Actionables:**
    - Clearly define and state what stage the game is in (e.g., Pre-Alpha, not just "past prototype").
    - Apply user feedback and explicitly mention the iteration process in the pitch.
    - Use the Pitchify Share URL for sending the deck.
    - Figure out exactly who at a publishing house you want to see the deck.
    - Answer all the Pitchify questions in your deck.
    - Clearly state "WHAT ARE YOU TRYING TO DO?" with the game.
    - Define "Must Haves" and "Nice to Haves" for your project features.

#### **Game Design & Development**

- **General Advice:**
    - The game's core loop must be clear.
    - Focus on the core gameplay. Don't let the project bloat.
    - Lean into creating a sandbox with joyous, player-created moments.
    - Continually ask if the core formula is working and if the pacing feels right.
- **Actionables:**
    - Tackle the trickiest development tasks as early as possible.
    - Make development milestones binary (done/not done) and broadly defined.
    - Look at similar products that successfully cross-pollinate genres.

#### **Community & Marketing**

- **General Advice:**
    - Market validation is crucial. You don't need huge numbers, just evidence of genuine interest.
    - Think about targeting communities and "places" online rather than just age/gender demographics.
    - Statistically, using the "Indie" tag can result in lower sales; position your marketing carefully.
- **Actionables:**
    - Use **GoTestify** for validated player feedback (suggested contact: Damian Isaac). Have clear questions for them to answer (e.g., "Is the core loop fun?").
    - Find the communities of games you are compared to (e.g., _Project Wingman_, _Helldivers 2_) and "bounce the game off" them to gauge interest.
    - Grow the Discord server and use the growing member count as a metric. Find a volunteer to moderate.
    - Continually ask the community for feedback and iterate based on it.
    - Ask your community/potential players key marketing questions: What games do they play? Where do they hang out online? Who are their favorite influencers? Where do they hear about new games?
    - Update the company website.
    - Set up a professional contact email (suggested: SOHO offers free emails with a domain).
    - Take lots of photos at events to use for social media.

#### **Team & Company Operations**

- **General Advice:**
    - Have clarity and thought in your internal processes. Set up a transparent and iterative pipeline.
    - Internal communication issues at the start can "snowball" into major problems later. Be transparent.
    - When giving feedback, never make it about the person; focus on the work.
    - Don't work in isolation; iterate the pitch on each other to see it from an outside perspective.
- **Actionables:**
    - Contact Pete and Orock (specific contacts mentioned).

### **Aimee Lingham @ ItalicPig**

#### **Business, Strategy & Finance**

- **General Advice:**
    - With no sales history, the publisher holds most of the negotiating power.
    - "Advance on Royalties" means you don't see royalty payments until the publisher has recouped their entire investment.
    - "Out of Developer Revenue" is a contract term that can apply to any stipulated cost, so scrutinize it.
- **Actionables:**
    - Conduct a thorough competitor analysis of the genre.
    - Create sales estimates for each competitor using hard numbers, potentially from **Steam Spy**.
    - Include rough revenue split calculations in your financial forecasts.
    - Create a dedicated "Publishing Spreadsheet" to track interactions, contacts, notes on personality, etc.
    - Talk to other studios who have worked with a publisher you're considering. Ask them about the experience.
    - Propose hypothetical scenarios to a publisher to fully understand the payback structure before signing.
    - Include potential employees and sustainment pay (post-launch support) in your budget.
    - Get all contract changes in writing.
    - Always have your next project planned out.

#### **Pitching & Decks**

- **General Advice:**
    - Don't pitch to publishers in December or January.
    - Tailor your pitch to the specific person/publisher you're talking to; exploit their personality traits if you can.
- **Actionables:**
    - Create a Game Design Document (GDD), Budget, Market Research, and a Trailer. Have different tiers of these for different publishers.
    - Address the team's inexperience directly in the pitch and explain how you are filling those gaps.
    - Show why the investment is a good value.
    - Exploit NI Screen's investment as leverage.

#### **Game Design & Development**

- **Actionables:**
    - Get the core gameplay locked down first and foremost.
    - Budget and plan for at least 3 months of post-launch bug fixing.

#### **Community & Marketing**

- **General Advice:**
    - Don't underestimate the power of a strong community.
    - A strong online presence can open the door to crowdfunding.
    - Don't be precious about what you show; get rid of the "bubble wrap."
    - LinkedIn is not an ideal platform for posting content for players.
- **Actionables:**
    - Create an "Influencer Cheat Sheet" to track relevant content creators.
    - Research how many content creators play games in your genre.
    - Make the community feel special and "official."
    - Create a roadmap leading up to a potential Steam Next Fest.
    - Investigate Bluesky as a potential social media platform.
    - Start creating "Vidocs" (video documentaries). Record meetings for B-roll footage and start posting "post-madness" development updates.
    - Create and cultivate "company lore" to make early supporters feel like part of the story.

### **Chris Filip @ BFI**

#### **Business, Strategy & Finance**

- **General Advice:**
    - The BFI can help with production timelines and GDDs.
    - It takes ~3-5 weeks to get BFI Certified and then an additional 4-6 weeks for HMRC processing.
    - You don't need an accountant for the initial BFI application, but you will need one for the final C&D (Compliance and Delivery).
    - Money from HMRC can take 2-4 months to arrive after approval.
    - Be prepared to self-publish your game as a backup plan.
- **Actionables:**
    - Create a clear plan for the Develop conference: define your goals and what you want to achieve there.
    - Use any "work for hire" history as leverage.
    - Apply for BFI Content Funding and explore their business loans.
    - Assess what the team is good at and what tasks can be delegated.
